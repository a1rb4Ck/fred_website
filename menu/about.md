Fred Nagorny is a french animator.
He has taught 2D and 3D animation production, character design and motion design at Gobelins Paris since 1988, as well as in other french professional schools ([Arts Décoratifs Paris ENSAD](https://www.ensad.fr/fr), [University of Marne-la-Vallée Gustave Eiffel](https://www.univ-gustave-eiffel.fr/), [INA](https://www.ina.fr/), [AFIDE](https://www.emagister.fr/afide-en54773.htm), ADIS, [Gobelins Annecy](https://www.lespapeteries.com/les-formations.php)).

He began animation at an early age, using a custom bench-title and his parents Super8 camera to produce shorts, studying motion design from cartoons to realistic effects.
After graduating as an architect, he started in the industry as a layout man, then moved to lead 2d animation productions in France, [Paris](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9lokapi) and [Montpellier](https://histoire3d.siggraph.org/index.php/Label_35).

In the late 80's, he applied 2d animation methods, like keyframes and tweens, to the new computer generated 3d animation medium.
He teamed-up with [Raymond Perrin](https://histoire3d.siggraph.org/index.php/Raymond_Perrin) to create the [Character software](https://histoire3d.siggraph.org/index.php/Character) to support his vision.
That leaded the [Relief studio](https://histoire3d.siggraph.org/index.php/Relief) to produce award-winning short films, like Le Pantin, and multiple commissioned films.
Relief's [Character software](https://histoire3d.siggraph.org/index.php/Character) was eventually integrated into [Softimage](https://histoire3d.siggraph.org/index.php/Softimage) and has contributed to numerous films.
